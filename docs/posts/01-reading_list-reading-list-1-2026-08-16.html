Construction costs climbed 7.4% in a single year, and the same week Bank of America put $250 billion on the table for infrastructure. Every dollar of that pledge buys less than it did twelve months ago.

Ground under the Port of Long Beach doesn't hold twice its own strength naturally — engineers gave it a grid. The port, second-busiest container seaport in the US, sits on soil that would otherwise need deep piles or full excavation to carry crane and container loads. A grid system doubles the ground's strength instead, which is the kind of fix a foreman notices because it means less digging, not more. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

Neros just raised $250 million to get defense drones into the field by the end of 2026. The money's going into the Archer AI and Bandit platform, built specifically for multi-asset control — one operator running a swarm, not one drone at a time. Watch this compute-per-drone ratio; it's the same math jobsite robotics will eventually run. https://www.therobotreport.com/neros-technologies-raises-250m-to-deploy-its-defense-drones-by-the-end-of-2026/

Bank of America is putting $250 billion behind US infrastructure — lending, banking, investing, and advising on data centers, power generation, and transportation. That's real capital stacked against real assets, not a press-conference number. Where it actually lands — grid, generation, or transit — is the story worth tracking through next quarter. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/bank-of-america-pledges-250b-toward-us-infrastructure-update/827898/

New Orleans didn't drown in 2005 because Katrina overtopped the levees. The failure was a specific structural flaw in the design itself — the storm was strong, but the levees broke before the water ever got the chance to just spill over. Worth the read if you think "built to code" and "built to survive" are the same thing. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

Construction costs rose 7.4% year-over-year in July. Economists are flagging more input price jumps ahead, especially fuel and materials — so this isn't a peak, it's a checkpoint. Budget accordingly. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/construction-costs-producer-price-index-july/827933/
