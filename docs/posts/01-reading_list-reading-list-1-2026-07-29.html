Reading list 1 — 2026-07-29

2026-07-29 · reading_list

Two out of three reaction wheels just died on a satellite named Link — mid-rescue, in orbit, spinning with only one wheel left holding it steady. No tow truck for that job.

Swift rescue mission loses control, literally. Link, the spacecraft sent to rescue NASA's Swift observatory, is now down to one working reaction wheel out of three. Preliminary investigation just confirmed two are dead — a builder's version of losing two legs on a tripod and hoping the third holds the load. https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/07/reaction-wheel-failures-leave-swift-rescue-mission-spinning-in-orbit/

A dam built in 1931 just got a $50 million facelift. Granite Construction won the spillway replacement at PG&E's Tiger Creek Regulator Dam in Amador County, California — rebuilding the crest structure and chute on a slab-and-buttress dam that's been standing since before World War II. Ninety-five years of service, one new contract to keep it standing. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/granite-construction-PGE-california-dam/826374/

New Orleans didn't flood because the storm was too big. It flooded because of a design flaw. Grady Hillhouse's breakdown of the August 2005 Katrina levee failures walks through the specific engineering choice that turned a strong hurricane into a catastrophe — not an act of God, an act of drafting. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

Building a bridge over the Mississippi took years. Tearing down the old one next to it was the harder job. The Iowa-Illinois Memorial Bridges opened in 2022, carrying I-74 between Moline and Bettendorf — but the demolition of the old span turned into its own engineering problem, worked out in detail here. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/6/16/how-to-demolish-a-bridge

If you don't run a national meteorology bureau, you don't get a weather radar. So someone built their own. Hackaday's writeup covers a DIY portable weather radar build — the kind of scrappy field-tool engineering that belongs on a jobsite trailer as much as a hobbyist's bench. https://hackaday.com/2026/07/29/building-a-portable-weather-radar/
