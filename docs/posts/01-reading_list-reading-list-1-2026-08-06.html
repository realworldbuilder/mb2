A rocket engine, a levee, and a container yard all failed the same way: something built to spec broke anyway. Five stories, all about the gap between the drawing and the dirt.

Blue Origin's New Glenn didn't blow up from bad luck — it blew up from a valve. The May static-fire failure traces back to one component in the BE-4 engine, the same engine stack that's supposed to be flying reused boosters routinely by now. One part, one explosion, one rocket program's timeline reset. https://spacenews.com/new-glenn-explosion-linked-to-rocket-engine-valve/

The New Orleans levees didn't fail from a storm too big to handle — they failed from a flaw nobody caught. Hurricane Katrina in August 2005 was strong, but the flood wasn't inevitable; it came down to a specific design defect in the system built to stop exactly that kind of water. Builders spend careers chasing tolerances on paper — this is the bill when one gets missed. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

A grid buried in the ground at the Port of Long Beach doubles the strength of the soil under it. The port is the country's second-busiest container terminal, moving freight for the entire west coast — reinforcing what's underneath isn't cosmetic, it's the difference between a terminal that settles and one that doesn't. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

Nashville's city council just used eminent domain to kill a data center project near the zoo. 235 points and 293 comments on Hacker News says this fight over land use and power draw is bigger than one lot — data centers are running into the same zoning walls that used to be reserved for landfills and prisons. https://www.costar.com/article/970809918/nashville-council-approves-eminent-domain-action-to-halt-data-center-project

New York City is the most densely populated place in the country, and it's packed underground to match — subways, water mains, steam pipes, foundations stacked on top of each other with no master map. Anyone who's had a dig turn into an archaeology project knows what "unlike any other city" means in practice. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/21/whats-under-your-feet-in-new-york-city
