Reading list 1 — 2026-08-14

2026-08-14 · reading_list

A house design just cut child malaria by 44 percent — no drugs, no nets, just better walls and a raised floor.

Star Homes, Tanzania. Kids living in these houses had 44% fewer malaria cases than kids in mud-and-thatch homes next door, plus lower rates of diarrhoea and respiratory infections. Published in Nature Medicine — which means somebody ran the numbers on architecture as a public health intervention, not a design award. The takeaway for builders: screened openings and elevated floors are cheap compared to a hospital bill. https://www.dezeen.com/2026/08/14/star-homes-malaria-study-tanzania/

Port of Long Beach. A layer of plastic mesh buried under the second-busiest container port in the U.S. doubles the strength of the ground it sits on. Geogrid works by locking soil particles in place so they can't shift sideways under load — cheaper than hauling in better dirt, and it's holding up cranes and container stacks that weigh a lot more than your average parking lot. Grady Hillhouse's breakdown is worth the read if you've ever wondered what's actually under a heavy-load pad. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

New Orleans, 2005. Hurricane Katrina was one of the strongest storms to ever hit a U.S. coastline — but that's not why the city flooded. Hillhouse walks through the actual levee design flaw that failed, the kind of detail that doesn't show up in the news footage. Worth a rewatch for anyone who thinks "it was just a big storm" is the whole story. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

Somewhere over China, this week. One of the country's workhorse rockets exploded mid-flight, and the engine under suspicion — the YF-100 — powers a big chunk of China's active launch fleet. If that engine is the root cause, this isn't a one-off bad day, it's a fleet-wide question mark. https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/08/one-of-chinas-workhorse-rockets-just-exploded-in-flight/

A $10 pair of calipers, cracked open. DiodeGoneWild tore down a cheap plastic digital caliper to see what's actually inside the tool half the trade carries in a pocket. No dramatic numbers here, just a good reminder that the instrument reading "0.01mm" on the screen was built to a budget — worth knowing before you trust it on something that matters. https://hackaday.com/2026/08/13/tearing-down-a-cheap-digital-caliper/
