Reading list 1 — 2026-09-12

2026-09-12 · reading_list

A commuter train packed with 250 people rolled into Hoboken Terminal in 2016 and just kept going — no brakes, no stop, straight through the end of the track.

A New Jersey Transit train failed to stop on September 29, 2016, overriding the bumper block at Hoboken Terminal during rush hour. About 250 passengers were aboard. Grady Hillhouse's breakdown of what actually stops a train — and what didn't that morning — is worth the read for anyone who thinks "fail-safe" means what it sounds like. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/9/1/stopping-the-unstoppable

Direct labor eats roughly half the cost of building a house. Cars get off with 6 to 8%. Construction Physics lays out why automation keeps failing to close that gap — the industry that should want robots the most has the hardest time using them. Worth reading before you believe the next jobsite robot demo. https://www.construction-physics.com/p/where-has-construction-automation

The Pentagon got rebuilt after 9/11 ahead of schedule and under budget — with zero disruption to the people still working inside it. Integrated project delivery and workforce planning did the heavy lifting, not heroics. A rare case where "ahead of schedule" isn't fiction — worth studying for the sequencing alone. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/lessons-learned-from-rebuilding-the-pentagon-after-911/830175/

Antares just landed $161 million from the DoD to build nuclear power for space — the largest space nuclear award the Pentagon has handed out. No reactor flying yet, just the contract. But space habitats need power that doesn't depend on sunlight, and nuclear is the only thing that scales. https://payloadspace.com/antares-wins-161m-award-for-space-nuclear-power/

3 gigawatts of data center load vanished off the grid in seconds — and it wasn't the first time. MIT Tech Review traces it back to Ashburn, Virginia, the world's largest data center cluster, where a transmission fault did the damage in July 2026. Two years earlier, one failed surge arrester dropped roughly 60 Virginia facilities at once. Same cluster, same weak point, twice. https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/10/1141649/powering-ai-is-an-architecture-problem/

Still watching: Ashburn's grid — third appearance this month, still no fix mentioned, just a repeat failure mode two years apart.
