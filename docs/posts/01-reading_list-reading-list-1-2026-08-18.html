Reading list 1 — 2026-08-18

2026-08-18 · reading_list

Amazon tracks the books it destroys with hidden AirTags — while training an AI on what's inside them. The team doing it uses a T. rex eating a book as its logo. Nobody's hiding the joke.

A hidden AirTag followed a shipment of rare books straight into a shredder. Amazon's AI training operation is chewing through physical texts fast enough that someone felt the need to track where the evidence goes — and slow enough that a tracker survived the trip to tell the story. https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/hidden-airtag-reveals-amazon-is-trashing-rare-books-to-train-ai/

$1.9 billion buys 6.7 miles of light rail in Van Nuys. Skanska and Stacy Witbeck just won the joint venture contract to build the line at grade for LA Metro. Do the math on that per-mile number before you sign anything with "at grade" in the fine print. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/skanska-stacy-witbeck-win-california-light-rail/827992/

California's high-speed rail authority needs $1 billion a year for 20 years, and even that won't arrive fast enough. The state's own inspector general says the project could run dry by the end of 2027. Twenty years of state infusions and the math still doesn't close — that's not a funding gap, that's a funding chasm. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/california-high-speed-rail-inspector-general-finances/827998/

New Orleans didn't lose the fight to Hurricane Katrina's wind speed — it lost to a design flaw in the levees themselves. Katrina was one of the strongest storms to ever hit a US coastline, by many measures. The failure in August 2005 traced back to how the levees were built, not just how hard the storm blew. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

Geogrid doubles the strength of the ground under the Port of Long Beach, the second-busiest container seaport in the US. No new soil, no deeper piles — just a grid layer doing the work dirt alone can't. Worth knowing before you spec a foundation on soft ground anywhere near a coastline. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground
