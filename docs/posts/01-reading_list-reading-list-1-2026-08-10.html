Reading list 1 — 2026-08-10

2026-08-10 · reading_list

$845 million to buy a drone company. Procore just told you where construction software money is actually going — not project management, not scheduling. Robotics data.

Procore acquired DroneDeploy for $845 million. They're not buying drones — they're buying the management layer that sits between jobsite robots and the office. Whoever owns that layer owns the next decade of contech. https://contechroundup.substack.com/p/why-procore-acquired-dronedeploy

Geogrid doubles the strength of the ground underneath it. The Port of Long Beach — second-busiest container seaport in the country — uses the stuff under its container yards so the soil can carry loads it never could on its own. No pile driving, no excavation, just a grid layer doing the work of doubling bearing capacity. Worth knowing before you spec a yard the old-fashioned way. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

The 2005 New Orleans levee failure traced back to a design flaw, not just the storm. Hurricane Katrina was genuinely one of the strongest hurricanes to ever hit the U.S. coast — but the water finding its way through wasn't purely a wind-speed problem. Worth the read if you've ever assumed "built to code" means "built right." https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

Blue Origin traced its catastrophic rocket accident to the main oxygen valve on one BE-4 engine. One valve, one engine, one anomaly — that's the whole root cause so far. Tolerance is truth: on a rocket, the gap between "spec" and "as-built" isn't measured in inches, it's measured in catastrophic failure. https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/08/blue-origin-narrowing-in-on-root-cause-of-catastrophic-rocket-accident/

Someone built a trebuchet that launches projectiles faster than the speed of sound. Medieval siege engineers never got there — turns out it just took a few hundred years and a hacker with a shop. No word on where it lands, but no idea how they kept it from destroying itself on the release. https://hackaday.com/2026/08/09/creating-a-supersonic-trebuchet/
