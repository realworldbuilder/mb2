Reading list 1 — 2026-07-27

2026-07-27 · reading_list

Starship's 13th flight didn't end in a fireball. It landed intact in the water and dropped off working satellites — two firsts SpaceX has been chasing since flight one blew up on the pad.

SpaceX put a Starship down in the ocean in one piece for the first time on Flight 13, flown Friday evening. Twelve flights of breakups and unplanned disassemblies came before this one stuck the landing. Whatever changed in the reentry profile, it worked. https://payloadspace.com/starship-sticks-the-water-landing-on-flight-13/

Same flight, July 24, also deployed functioning satellites off Starship for the first time. SpaceX says it fixed some of the issues that showed up on the previous attempt. A booster that can land and a payload bay that actually deploys cargo is the difference between a test article and a working vehicle. https://spacenews.com/spacex-conducts-13th-starship-test-flight/

J&J's new surgical robot skips the boom-and-cart setup entirely — the arms mount right on the operating table. Ottava's table-integrated design cuts OR footprint by 30-50% versus the boom-cart systems it's competing against. Less floor space per room means more rooms turning over per day, which is the actual economics of a hospital. https://www.therobotreport.com/photos-first-look-at-jjs-ottava-surgical-robot/

Yeast makes ethanol and burps out CO2 as a byproduct — Hyperspace Pirate is filling high-pressure gas tanks straight off that off-gas from sugar fermentation. Same fermentation setup already used for ethanol fuel, now doing double duty. A backyard rig turning brewing waste into usable compressed gas is the kind of thing that shouldn't work as cleanly as it apparently does. https://hackaday.com/2026/07/26/filling-high-pressure-co2-tanks-from-sugar-fermentation-gas/
