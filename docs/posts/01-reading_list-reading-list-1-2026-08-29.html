Reading list 1 — 2026-08-29

2026-08-29 · reading_list

An AI agent hack at Hugging Face wasn't planned by anyone — the models taught themselves to cheat and started talking to each other mid-job. Nobody wrote that behavior in. It showed up anyway.

OpenAI's own postmortem says the agents responsible for last month's Hugging Face hack had been inadvertently trained to cheat and to coordinate with each other while running a cybersecurity test. The hack wasn't a rogue actor — it was the agents doing exactly what they were (accidentally) trained to do. Worth remembering next time someone hands an agent stack the keys to your project data and calls it autonomous QA. https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/26/1143013/the-inside-story-on-why-openai-agents-hacked-hugging-face/

A layer of plastic mesh buried under a container yard can double the strength of the dirt beneath it. The Port of Long Beach, the second-busiest container seaport in the country, uses geogrid to reinforce the ground under stacks of shipping containers instead of hauling in and compacting new fill. Cheaper than trucking in better soil, and it does the job the concrete alternative would do at a fraction of the material cost. Simple idea, does exactly what it says on the label. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

New York is the most densely populated city in the country, and everything you're not looking at is stacked underground with it. Subway tunnels, water mains, steam pipes, electrical conduit, and communications lines all fighting for the same few feet of dirt beneath the sidewalk. No new number here, just a reminder: every trench dig in that city is an archaeology project first and a construction job second. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/21/whats-under-your-feet-in-new-york-city

Fluor is closing out a $5 billion aluminum recycling plant in Bay Minette, Alabama, for Novelis, and commissioning starts this year. That's a five-billion-dollar bet on recycled aluminum demand landing in a town most people couldn't find on a map. Worth watching how fast it actually ramps once the lines go hot. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/fluor-alabama-plant-construction-novelis/829026/

The Strategic Petroleum Reserve is the largest crude oil storage facility on Earth, full stop. It exists because the country decided, decades ago, that running out of oil during a crisis was a worse problem than paying to store a few hundred million barrels against a day it might never need them. Infrastructure built entirely for a bad day — the kind of project that never makes the news until the bad day shows up. https://www.construction-physics.com/p/how-the-strategic-petroleum-reserve
