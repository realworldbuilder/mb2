Starship spent 24 days floating in the Indian Ocean before anyone got it back to shore. Now a team of SpaceX engineers is combing over a spacecraft that sat in salt water for over three weeks — corrosion doesn't care about your launch schedule.

A booster that fell out of the sky landed itself again — twice in a row now. Landspace stuck the first-stage landing on Zhuque-3 Tuesday, the second successful recovery in as many orbital launches from the Gobi Desert. China now has a reusable rocket program with back-to-back landings on the board, not just one lucky catch. https://spacenews.com/chinas-landspace-recovers-booster-with-second-orbital-launch-of-zhuque-3-rocket/

The second-busiest container port in the country is standing on dirt that's twice as strong as it used to be. The Port of Long Beach uses ground grid reinforcement to double soil strength under the yard — no new land, no new seawall, just geogrid doing the work underground. Worth knowing if you've ever wondered why a container crane doesn't sink into reclaimed fill. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

$5.3 billion just went out the door to fix places where trains and trucks meet. The Federal Railway Administration is closing more than 30 grade crossings outright and upgrading over 1,000 more with new infrastructure and tech. That's a lot of intersections where somebody's flagging traffic getting redesigned out of the job. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/dot-rail-package-funds-crossings-infrastructure/828145/

A rocket part left a fresh scar on the Moon, and NASA got the after-photos a year later. A Falcon 9 upper stage hit the lunar surface on Aug. 5, 2025 after its January 2025 Blue Ghost 1 launch, and NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter imaged the new crater Aug. 11-12. Spent hardware doesn't just disappear — it leaves a hole somebody eventually has to explain. https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/moon/nasas-lro-images-falcon-9-crater-on-moon-learns-new-details/
