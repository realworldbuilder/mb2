2026-08-26 · reading_list

$100 billion for a spaceport in Louisiana — more than the other four projects on today's list combined, and then some.

A layer of plastic mesh buried under a container yard can double the strength of the dirt beneath it. The Port of Long Beach, the second-busiest container seaport in the country, is using geogrid soil reinforcement to keep the ground under West Coast import traffic from failing under load. No excavation, no dig-and-replace — just grid, then gravel, then go. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

SpaceX wants to put up to $100 billion into a spaceport in Louisiana. The company's own line on it: "This will be a project like no other." No timeline or site plan in hand yet — just the number, and it's a big enough number that everyone in the Gulf Coast supply chain should be paying attention. https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/08/spacex-intends-to-invest-up-to-100-billion-in-massive-louisiana-spaceport/

$9.2 billion just got assigned to a Tennessee interstate project, and it's the largest public-private partnership concession for an interstate choice lanes project in U.S. history. Gov. Bill Lee's office confirmed the construction team pick. Choice lanes P3s are still rare enough in the U.S. that a deal this size resets what "big" means for the model. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/92b-tennessee-highway-project-picks-construction-team/828758/

NYC is putting $4 billion into fixing a highway it's calling "deteriorating" — the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway. Work starts in 2030 and runs 10 years, targeting aging concrete and the triple-cantilever section that's been a known liability for decades. A decade-long repair job on a road that's carrying traffic the entire time. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/nyc-brooklyn-queens-expressway-project/828737/

Kiewit's president of nuclear solutions is calling out a $1 billion opportunity in nuclear construction. Mike Rinehart's take covers the resurgence in nuclear builds, project execution, and the headwinds still standing in the way. Nuclear construction hasn't had a real growth story in the U.S. in years — worth watching who actually breaks ground first. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/kiewit-exec-billion-opportunity-nuclear-construction/828695/
