A plastic grid buried under a container terminal at the Port of Long Beach doubles the strength of the ground it sits on — no piles, no dredging, just netting and rock.

The world's largest crude oil stockpile isn't a country's national reserve — it's a stretch of salt caverns and tank farms Americans just call the SPR. It's the biggest crude storage facility on the planet, full stop, and most of it lives underground in Gulf Coast salt domes built for exactly this. Worth knowing next time gas prices become dinner-table news. https://www.construction-physics.com/p/how-the-strategic-petroleum-reserve

Geogrid — a plastic mesh laid into the ground — doubles the bearing strength of soil at the Port of Long Beach, the second-busiest container port in the country. No new concrete, no new piles, just a layer of grid holding the rock in place so heavier loads don't punch through. The port sits next to LA's port too, so this is ground that has to hold up under container stacks moving around the clock. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground
