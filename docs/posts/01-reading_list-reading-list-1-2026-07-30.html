New Orleans flooded in August 2005, but the storm wasn't the failure — the levees were built with a flaw nobody caught until it was too late.

Katrina wasn't a natural disaster. It was an engineering one. New analysis lays out the design flaw baked into the New Orleans levee system before Hurricane Katrina ever made landfall in August 2005 — the storm just found the weak point that was already there. Worth the read if you think "act of God" ever fully covers a failure; usually there's a drawing somewhere with the mistake on it. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

L3Harris is pushing its missile business IPO back to 2027, even with defense demand surging right now. The CEO's argument: the market isn't pricing the Pentagon-backed business correctly, so why sell into a bad number. A reminder that "surging demand" and "good time to IPO" aren't the same sentence. https://spacenews.com/l3harris-delays-missile-business-ipo-to-2027-despite-surging-defense-demand/
