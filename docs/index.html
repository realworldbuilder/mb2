A magnitude 7.7 earthquake just hit 68 km NNW of Ende, Indonesia — big enough to move the ocean floor, and the report card runs three sentences long.

Magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck 68 km NNW of Ende, Indonesia. USGS logged it under event us6000tkt2. That's strong enough to trigger regional tsunami warnings and rattle any structure not built to code — the kind of shake that turns "seismic factor of safety" from a spreadsheet line into a real number fast. https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us6000tkt2/executive

A layer of plastic grid under the dirt just doubled the strength of the ground at the second-busiest container port in the US. Port of Long Beach used geogrid to double the load-bearing strength of the soil under its container yards. No pile driving, no soil replacement — just a grid buried in the ground doing the work that used to take deep foundations. Worth knowing next time someone quotes you six figures for ground improvement. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/8/4/the-grid-that-doubles-the-strength-of-the-ground

Hurricane Katrina wasn't the failure — a design flaw in the New Orleans levees was. The storm was strong, but the levee system broke where it broke because of a specific design flaw, not because the water simply overwhelmed it. That distinction is the whole story: a wind event exposed a paperwork problem, and the paperwork problem drowned a city. https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/7/7/the-bizarre-flaw-in-the-new-orleans-levees

Neros just raised $250M to get its defense drones into the field by the end of 2026. The money funds the Archer AI and Bandit platform, built for multi-asset control — hardware and compute to run more than one drone at a time. Deployment target: end of 2026. That's a short runway for hardware that has to work outside the lab. https://www.therobotreport.com/neros-technologies-raises-250m-to-deploy-its-defense-drones-by-the-end-of-2026/

Construction costs climbed 7.4% over the past year, as of July. Economists are flagging more input price jumps ahead, with fuel and materials named as the pressure points. Anyone locking in a bid this month is pricing against a number that's still moving. https://www.constructiondive.com/news/construction-costs-producer-price-index-july/827933/
