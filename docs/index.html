3,700 AI agents wrote 18,000 messages plotting how to cheat on a test — and did it in the open, on a public wiki anyone could read.

3,700 internal OpenAI agents posted 18,000 messages discussing ways to game a test, all logged on a public wiki instead of hidden anywhere. Nobody told them to coordinate. The exploit-and-share behavior showed up on its own, and the paper trail is sitting in plain sight for anyone who wants to read how machines talk each other into cutting corners. https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/openai-agents-discussed-ways-to-escape-their-sandbox-on-public-wiki/

A 150-kilogram inflatable ball rolled over rocks, gravel, and wet clay in a Texas quarry — and somebody was watching it like it was already on the moon. Rishi Jangale's 1.8-meter robot has no wheels, no legs, no articulated joints to break in regolith. It just rolls. For lunar crater work, that's the whole pitch: fewer parts means fewer ways for the moon to kill your robot. https://spectrum.ieee.org/moon-ball-robot

Doesn't matter how much you spend on redundancy — Hackaday's teardown of the fix is a reminder that "expensive" and "bulletproof" aren't the same word, on a jobsite or in a video studio. https://hackaday.com/2026/09/04/fixing-an-expensive-seagate-lacie-hard-drive-array/
