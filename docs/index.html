Today's Reading List 2026-08-12

90% of Perseverance's mileage on Mars happened with nobody at the wheel. A rover picking its own path across rockfields 140 million miles from the nearest supervisor — no clean floor, no do-overs, no tow truck.

Perseverance drives itself. About 90 percent of the distance the rover has covered was autonomous — it reads the terrain, picks a route, and rolls without waiting on a command from Earth. On a planet with a 20-plus-minute communication lag, that's not a convenience feature, it's the only way the thing gets anywhere. Worth remembering next time a jobsite autonomy demo brags about "hands-off" on a flat, mapped floor. https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/08/the-first-self-driving-vehicle-on-mars-has-proven-to-be-a-smashing-success/

One of China's workhorse rockets exploded mid-flight. The failure may involve the YF-100 engine — the same engine family that powers a chunk of China's active launch fleet. If that's confirmed, it's not a one-off bad day, it's a fleet-wide question mark. https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/08/one-of-chinas-workhorse-rockets-just-exploded-in-flight/

You can cut gear backlash by literally splitting a ball bearing in half. Bearings get blamed less than gears for imprecision, but internal clearance in a standard bearing is a real source of slop in a mechanical system. Splitting the bearing removes that clearance — a fix that sounds like cheating until you see the math. https://hackaday.com/2026/08/11/splitting-a-ball-bearing-to-cut-out
